# Weiss2009 - Atypical Memory B Cells Expanded in Malaria-Endemic Area

**Full citation:** Weiss, G. E., Crompton, P. D., Li, S., Walsh, L. A., Moir, S., Traore, B., Kayentao, K., Ongoiba, A., Doumbo, O. K., & Pierce, S. K. (2009). Atypical memory B cells are greatly expanded in individuals living in a malaria-endemic area. *The Journal of Immunology*, *183*(3), 2176–2182. https://doi.org/10.4049/jimmunol.0901297

**Raw file:** `[[raw/weiss2009.pdf]]`

## Summary

This is the paper that put "atypical memory B cells" into the malaria literature, and — by explicit analogy to Moir's HIV work published the year before — that carried the **exhaustion** framing across from HIV into parasitic infection. Using four-colour flow cytometry on cryopreserved PBMC from an age-stratified subset of a Malian cohort, the authors show that a CD19⁺CD27⁻CD21⁻CD20⁺CD10⁻ population is expanded roughly **ten-fold** in lifelong *Plasmodium falciparum*-exposed Malians relative to malaria-naive US adults, in children as young as two years, with a trend of further increase with age.

They then characterise the population against the inhibitory- and homing-receptor signature that Moir had used to call the HIV cells exhausted, and find it reproduced: FCRL4, CD85j and CD22 up; CD11c and CXCR3 up; CXCR4, CXCR5, CCR7 and CD62L down. Finally, in what the paper itself calls **"preliminary studies"**, bead-fractionated atypical MBCs fail to differentiate into IgG-secreting cells under polyclonal stimulation that drives classical MBCs efficiently. On that combined basis the authors conclude the cells "both phenotypically and functionally resemble exhausted MBCs," and propose that their high prevalence may contribute to the delayed acquisition and short-lived nature of malaria humoral immunity.

Two things about the paper matter more to this wiki than its headline. First, **the functional half — the part the field remembers — is n=1 and n=2**, with the n=2 arm cited to unpublished data, and it uses a different, coarser gate than the phenotyping. The first-party evidence that malaria atypical MBCs cannot become ASCs is [[Portugal2015 - Malaria Atypical MBCs Lack BCR Signaling and Effector Function]] (n=9–14), not this paper. Second, **the FCRL4 result was later withdrawn by the same group** as antibody cross-reactivity. What survives intact, and is what this paper should be cited for, is the expansion itself and the receptor panel in Fig. 4 — which already contained the CD11c⁺CXCR3⁺CXCR5⁻ signature five years before the DN2 literature named it.

## Study Design

- **Type:** Prospective observational cohort (passive malaria surveillance) with cross-sectional flow phenotyping at a single timepoint, plus preliminary in vitro differentiation assays. Not an intervention study.
- **Sample size:** **n=87** age-stratified subset randomly selected from a 225-person cohort. ⚠ **The flow denominators are smaller than the cohort:** Fig. 2 reports Malian adults **n=14**, children 8–10 y **n=25**, children 2–7 y **n=36** (75 total) against **n=10** US adults. Receptor panel (Fig. 4) **n=12** (6 children 2–4 y + 6 adults, paired). Fresh-PBMC control **n=16**. **ELISpot n=1 and n=2.**
- **Setting:** Kambila, Mali — rural village of ~1500, intense seasonal *Pf* transmission beginning June, peaking October, ending December. 8-month analysis window May–December 2006. PBMC drawn **before** the season (May 2006) and at **peak** transmission (October 2006), cryopreserved in liquid nitrogen; phenotyping performed on the October draw. Comparators: 10 healthy anonymous adult blood-bank donors in the United States (travel history unavailable; prior *Pf* exposure judged unlikely).
- **Population:** Ages 2–25 at enrolment; groups 2–4 y (n=35), 5–7 y (n=11), 8–10 y (n=25), 18–25 y (n=16); 54.0% female. Enrolment exclusions: Hb <7 g/dl, fever ≥37.5 °C, acute systemic illness, antimalarials or immunosuppressives within 30 days, pregnancy. **Asymptomatic *Pf* parasitaemia was not exclusionary and was not treated** — 12.6% (11/87) smear-positive at enrolment, geometric mean 770 parasites/µl. Sickle-cell trait (HbAS) 9.4%; GI helminth 10.5%; urinary schistosomiasis 9.5%. Malaria incidence over the season 1.44 ± 1.32 episodes; 67.8% had ≥1 episode; 3 severe episodes.

## Key Findings

**★ The expansion — the result that survives everything else**

- **Atypical MBCs: Malian adults mean 15.5% (95% CI 9.7–21.2) of total CD19⁺ B cells vs US adults 1.6% (95% CI 1.0–2.2), p < 0.001.** Malian children **9.8% (95% CI 8.2–11.3)**, p < 0.001 vs US adults (Fig. 2E, Wilcoxon rank-sum).
- A **trend of increasing atypical MBC frequency with increasing age** among Malian donors — i.e. with cumulative lifetime exposure, not with acute infection.
- **Not a cryopreservation artefact.** Fresh PBMC from 16 adults at the same site gave **14.1% (95% CI 8.1–20.1)**, closely matching the frozen-sample figure.
- **Activated MBCs (CD27⁺CD21⁻CD20⁺CD10⁻) also expanded**, far less dramatically: Malian adults 3.7% (95% CI 2.5–5.0) vs US adults 1.3% (95% CI 0.6–2.1), p = 0.001; Malian children 1.9% (95% CI 1.4–2.4), p < 0.001 vs Malian adults (Fig. 2F).
- **Total B cells are unchanged across the season.** CD19⁺ cells per PBMC: **12.16% (95% CI 11.36–12.97) before** vs **11.71% (95% CI 10.86–12.57) at peak**, **p = 0.451** — the atypical expansion is a compositional shift within a stable B cell compartment, not lymphocytosis.
- Naive B cells and plasma cells/plasmablasts were broadly similar between US and Malian donors (Fig. 2A, 2B). **Immature (CD10⁺) B cells appeared lower in Malian adults** than in US volunteers or Malian children (Fig. 2C, p < 0.001). Classical MBCs were similar between Malian and US adults; the 2–7 and 8–10 y groups had a smaller proportion (p = 0.023, p = 0.008), which the authors read as **age-related and unrelated to malaria**.

**The gating strategy, and what it does and does not resolve**

- **Four-colour, two-stain panel on a FACSCalibur**, analysed in FlowJo. Stain 1: FITC-CD21, PE-CD20, PECy7-CD19, APC-CD10. Stain 2: FITC-CD21, PE-IgG, PECy7-CD19, APC-CD27.
- Within CD19⁺ (Fig. 1): **naive** CD27⁻CD21⁺CD10⁻; **classical MBC** CD27⁺CD21⁺CD10⁻; **atypical MBC** CD27⁻CD21⁻CD20⁺CD10⁻; **activated MBC** CD27⁺CD21⁻CD20⁺CD10⁻; **immature** CD10⁺; **plasma cells/blasts** CD27⁺CD21⁻CD20⁻.
- ⚠ **Subset frequencies are computed by arithmetic subtraction across two separate tubes**, not read off a single Boolean gate — atypical MBCs are reported as CD19⁺CD21⁻CD27⁻ **minus** CD19⁺CD21⁻CD10⁺ immature cells, and activated MBCs as CD19⁺CD27⁺CD21⁻ **minus** CD19⁺CD27⁺CD21⁻CD20⁻ plasma cells.
- ⚠ **A hard-coded 2% correction was applied to every donor.** Naive germinal-centre cells (CD19⁺CD21⁺CD10⁺CD27⁻) were taken to constitute ~2% of CD19⁺ cells — on the basis of **"S. Moir, unpublished observations"** — and that 2% was added back to each donor's naive count. A fixed constant, not a per-donor measurement.
- ⚠ **No IgD, no T-bet and no CD11c in the subset-defining panel.** CD11c and CXCR3 *are* measured (Fig. 4), but as characterisation markers on already-gated populations, not as gating parameters. See Relevance & Notes.

- **CD72** showed little difference between subpopulations (data not shown).
- The pattern was **similar in Malian children and adults**, and the authors state it is comparable to HIV-viraemic exhausted MBCs and to FCRL4⁺ tonsillar MBCs. Naive and classical MBC phenotypes matched those described for US individuals.

**★⚠ The FCRL4 result was later withdrawn by the same group**

- Weiss reports FCRL4 significantly elevated on atypical MBCs (Fig. 4A). The reagent is **M. Cooper's mouse anti-FCRL4 mAb**, detected **indirectly** with a **PE-conjugated rabbit anti-mouse IgG2a secondary** (Invitrogen) — a two-step stain, not a directly conjugated clone.
- [[Portugal2015 - Malaria Atypical MBCs Lack BCR Signaling and Effector Function]] finds FCRL4 **absent** from the same population at both mRNA (qPCR, n=4) and protein (flow, n=10) against a RAMOS-FcRL4⁺ positive control, and attributes this staining to **cross-reactivity of the FcRL4-specific mAb with FcRL5 or FcRL3**.
- ⚠ **This is a withdrawn marker claim, not a retracted paper.** The expansion result, the rest of Fig. 4 and the study design are unaffected. Per curator decision [2026-09-14] the correction is carried as an in-page ⚠ at the point of the claim rather than as a page-level retraction banner. The other Fig. 4 markers used directly conjugated commercial reagents (R&D Systems, BD, Invitrogen) and carry no comparable concern.

**Class switching (Fig. 3) — inferred, not sequenced**

- Surface IgG was measured on classical and atypical MBCs across three age bands. The **proportion IgG⁺ rises with age in both subsets**, and atypical MBCs were **somewhat more IgG⁺ than classical**: approximately 46% vs 31% (2–7 y), 64% vs 44% (8–10 y), 67% vs 45% (adults).
- The authors conclude that "the isotype-switching history is similar in the different MBC subpopulations" and that the high IgG⁺ proportion **"indicates that it is likely these cells have undergone somatic hypermutation."**
- ⚠ **SHM was never measured.** No BCR sequencing, no V-gene analysis and no mutation counting was performed in this paper. The SHM statement is an inference from isotype alone. See Relevance & Notes.

**★★ The functional result — the origin of the "exhausted" claim, at n=1 and n=2**

- Mature **CD10⁻** B cells were isolated by **negative magnetic bead selection** (StemCell enrichment cocktail supplemented with tetrameric anti-CD10), then split by a two-step magnetic bead process into **CD19⁺CD27⁻CD21^low^ (atypical)**, **CD19⁺CD27⁺CD21^high^ (classical)** and **CD19⁺CD27⁻CD21^high^ (naive)** fractions. Cultured 5 days, then IgG ELISpot on plates coated with goat anti-human IgG.
- **CpG + SAC (n=1):** classical MBC **34,900 ASC/10⁶** (SD ± 2,000); **atypical MBC 0/10⁶**; naive 1,300/10⁶ (SD ± 200).
- **Pokeweed mitogen + SAC + CpG + IL-10 (n=2, cited as "G. E. Weiss, unpublished data"):** classical **102,800/10⁶** (SD ± 18,200); **atypical 2,600/10⁶** (SD ± 1,200); naive 8,250/10⁶ (SD ± 2,000).
- Conclusion drawn: "By this criteria, the atypical MBCs in Malian volunteers are hyporesponsive to stimuli that activate classical MBCs, and thus both phenotypically and functionally resemble exhausted MBCs."
- ⚠ **Three limitations sit on top of each other here.** (i) The paper itself calls these **"preliminary studies"**; n=1 and n=2, with the quoted SDs being technical rather than biological replicates. (ii) The n=2 arm is attributed to **unpublished data**. (iii) **The functional fractions use a different gate from the phenotyping** — CD27⁻CD21^low^ after CD10 depletion, without the CD20⁺ refinement used in Fig. 1 — and were **bead-sorted, with no purity figures reported**.

**The negatives — including the one reported only as "data not shown"**

- **★ Atypical MBC frequency was not associated with clinical malaria risk.** "Neither the percentage of atypical MBCs before the malaria season nor the percentage at the peak of the malaria season was associated with risk of clinical malaria, as defined by malaria incidence or time to the first malaria episode (**data not shown**)." Age-adjusted Cox, logistic and Poisson models were pre-specified in the statistical methods for exactly this question.
- **Asymptomatic *Pf* parasitaemia:** children 2–10 y with parasitaemia at the end of the dry season (n=9) 14.7% (95% CI 2.7–21.0) vs without (n=62) 9.9% (95% CI 8.1–11.7), **p = 0.055** — and ⚠ **in multivariate regression including age as a covariate this association did not reach significance.** Per Decisions [2026-08-23] this unadjusted subgroup result is quarantined to this page and is not propagated as a finding.
- **Helminth infection:** with (n=8) 13.4% (95% CI 5.2–21.6) vs without (n=56) 9.9% (95% CI 8.1–11.7), **p = 0.291**; the authors note the sample may be too small.
- **Antigen specificity was never assessed.** The authors state they could not determine whether *Pf*-specific MBCs are differentially represented in the atypical compartment, because of the small cell numbers obtainable — especially from young children.
- HIV was **not tested for**; the authors argue from national prevalence (1.5%) that it is unlikely to explain the expansion. Malnutrition was not assessed.

**Authors' interpretation and speculation**

- Framed explicitly by analogy to Moir et al. 2008 (HIV): atypical MBCs may be "the end product of a defective pathway that normally functions to yield differentiated MBCs."
- Alternative offered in the same paragraph: the cells may be responsive to **other** stimuli and contribute to the short-lived antibody response by **giving rise to short-lived plasma cells**, or by interfering with the generation of long-lived plasma cells. *Pf*-specific antibodies rose across the season but were undetectable by the start of the next one (P. D. Crompton, unpublished).
- A third possibility is raised and not resolved: the expansion may **benefit the host**, since adults — who are largely clinically immune — carry the most atypical MBCs, and asymptomatic parasitaemia is itself associated with protection.

## Relevance & Notes

**★★ This ingest corrects a misattribution the wiki had been carrying: two experiments credited to Weiss 2009 that Weiss 2009 never performed.** Three wiki locations stated that malaria atypical MBCs are **IFN-γ-driven** and show **diverse V_H gene usage**, citing Weiss 2009. Read against `raw/cancro2020.pdf`, the review says something different: *"The expansion of a similar atypical memory cell subset among individuals living in malaria-endemic areas was reported by **Weiss et al. (94)** in 2009 (reviewed in 95). **Further findings from this group** revealed that these cells are driven by IFN-γ **(25)** and display a diverse V_H gene usage **(96)**"* — where ref 25 is **Obeng-Adjei 2017** (*PLoS Pathog*) and ref 96 is **Zinocker 2015** (*J Immunol*). The wiki compressed three citations into one.

This is the **second instance** of the review-compression class found on 2026-09-14, and a harder one than the first. The [[Portugal2015 - Malaria Atypical MBCs Lack BCR Signaling and Effector Function]] case dropped a hedge from a claim that was otherwise real; here the wiki asserted **experiments the cited primary does not contain** — this paper performs four-colour flow cytometry, magnetic bead fractionation and IgG ELISpot, and contains **no repertoire sequencing and no cytokine measurement of any kind**. The primary refutes the attribution directly rather than merely failing to support it. Corrected at [[Atypical B Cell]], [[BCR Sequencing]] and [[Cancro2020 - Age-Associated B Cells]]; logged as a Notable Finding.

**★ What this paper is the right citation for, and what it is not.** It is the right citation for the **existence and scale of the malaria atypical MBC expansion**, for its presence in children as young as two, and for the **Fig. 4 receptor panel**. It is a weak citation for **functional hyporesponsiveness** — that claim rests here on n=1 and n=2 preliminary ELISpot, and the first-party evidence is [[Portugal2015 - Malaria Atypical MBCs Lack BCR Signaling and Effector Function]] at n=9–14 with an ASC-differentiation assay, a sort-artefact control and an acute-infection arm. Per the wiki's evidence-weighting convention every propagated citation of the functional claim carries its n inline.

**★ The 2009 panel already contained the DN2 signature.** CD11c↑, CXCR3↑, CXCR5↓, CCR7↓, CD62L↓ on CD21⁻CD27⁻ cells is, marker for marker, the phenotype [[Jenks2018 - DN2 B Cells and EF Pathway in SLE]] describes in SLE nine years later and that [[DN2 B Cell]] is built on. The signature was in the infection literature first and was read through the **exhaustion** lens rather than the **effector** one — the interpretive frame, not the data, is what differed. Worth holding alongside [[Beckers2023 - Origins and Functions of DN B Cells]]'s split-by-disease-class reading: the same surface phenotype was called exhausted in chronic infection and activated in autoimmunity by investigators looking at overlapping marker sets.

**⚠ Gating caveat, per the wiki's standing IgD rule.** The subset-defining panel is CD19/CD20/CD21/CD27/CD10 — **no IgD**. Without it the CD27⁻CD21⁻CD20⁺CD10⁻ gate cannot exclude **IgD⁺ [[Activated Naive B Cell|activated naive]] cells**, which share CD27⁻CD21⁻ and are a substantial fraction of the CD21⁻ compartment in the SLE literature. This population is therefore **not demonstrably** the IgD⁻CD27⁻ [[Double-Negative B Cell|DN]] population the dengue spine concerns; it is *malaria-associated CD21⁻CD27⁻ atypical MBC*. The same caveat is already recorded for Portugal2015, which uses the identical gate. Note also that the gate is built by **subtraction across two tubes** and carries a **fixed 2% naive-GC correction** applied identically to every donor.

**⚠ The SHM claim on this page is inference from isotype.** The authors move from "high proportion of IgG⁺" to "likely these cells have undergone somatic hypermutation" in a single sentence. No sequencing was done. This is the third specificity-inflation hazard latent in the paper's own prose — alongside the FCRL4 reagent and the n=1 functional claim — and it is flagged rather than propagated: [[Somatic Hypermutation]] records it as an inference, not a measurement. Portugal2015 later measured SHM in this population directly (~3% for switched clones, indistinguishable from classical MBC), which is what the wiki should cite.

**On the label "FACS."** The paper writes "FACS analyses were performed on a FACSCalibur flow cytometer." A FACSCalibur is an analyser and cannot sort; all physical separation here was **magnetic**. [[FACS Sorting]] is deliberately **not** listed under Methods Used — this is the [2026-09-06] method mis-filing class (a method that sounds like the one performed) and the trim is applied at ingest rather than left for a later audit.

**Relation to the exhaustion framing more broadly.** The paper imports its interpretive vocabulary wholesale from Moir 2008 (HIV, **still un-ingested**) and from the exhausted-CD8 T cell literature. [[Cancro2020 - Age-Associated B Cells]] notes that the HIV cells, despite BCR hyporesponsiveness, **proliferate robustly to TLR9** — whereas here CpG (a TLR9 ligand) failed to drive ASC output. The readouts differ (proliferation vs terminal differentiation) so this is not a strict contradiction, but it is a reason not to treat "exhausted" as a single validated state across the two infections. Recorded on [[TLR9]] and [[Atypical B Cell Effector Output]] with the n=1/n=2 caveat attached.

**Limitations (authors' and curator's):**

- **Cross-sectional phenotyping.** Although the parent study is longitudinal with pre- and peak-season draws, the subset comparisons reported here are at the October timepoint; no within-individual trajectory of atypical MBC frequency is shown.
- **The comparator group is a convenience sample.** Ten anonymous US blood-bank donors, travel history unavailable, not age- or sex-matched to the Malian adults — and the largest reported differences are precisely the US-vs-Malian-adult ones.
- **The cohort denominator (n=87) is not the flow denominator (n=75).** The discrepancy is not explained in the text.
- **Four-colour panel.** Two markers plus CD19 and CD21 per tube; no dump channel, no viability dye described, and no FMO or isotype controls reported. Subset assignment depends on subtraction between two separately stained tubes.
- **Bead-sorted functional fractions with no reported purity**, and a gate that does not match the phenotyping gate.
- Curator's note: the paper reports **IgG ELISpot only** — IgM and IgA output were never assessed, so "failed to produce any ASCs" means *IgG*-secreting cells.

## Questions Raised

- **Does the expansion have any functional consequence for the host?** This paper finds no association with clinical malaria risk in either direction, at either timepoint, using age-adjusted models — and the authors raise, without resolving, that the cells may be protective, harmful or bystander. Sixteen years and a much larger literature later, this is still the open question.
- **What drives the expansion?** The authors exclude nothing: parasite antigen, other parasite products, host genetics, malnutrition and environmental co-factors are all left open. The asymptomatic-parasitaemia association was p = 0.055 and vanished on age adjustment.
- **Is the exhaustion analogy to HIV a real biological identity or a shared surface phenotype?** Portugal2015 later showed that malaria atypical MBCs are **FcRL4⁻** where HIV and tonsillar tissue-like MBCs are FcRL4⁺, and proposed that the immune environment dictates which FCRL family member is used. If one of the founding markers of the analogy does not hold, how much of the rest does?
- **Why does atypical MBC frequency increase with age in an endemic population while clinical immunity is also increasing?** The authors note the coincidence and suggest a possible host benefit. Nothing in the wiki's corpus tests it.
- **Does the compartment expand at the expense of the immature/transitional pool?** Immature (CD10⁺) B cells were lower in Malian adults; the paper does not pursue it, and the wiki has no source that examines whether chronic infection alters B cell output from the marrow.
